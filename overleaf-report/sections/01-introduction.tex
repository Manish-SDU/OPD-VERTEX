\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Med Flow was developed as part of the fourth-semester Software Engineering curriculum. It is a web-based consultation-support system for recording consultation audio, transcribing speech, generating draft clinical documentation, and supporting doctor review, PDF export, and follow-up communication. The project responds to the documented administrative burden created by electronic health record work, where documentation can consume a substantial part of the clinical day\refcite{1,6,7}.

The report focuses on the consultation and post-consultation workflow rather than a complete outpatient administration platform. A lightweight booking and scheduling flow was implemented to create appointment context inside the semester system, but the architecture still treats scheduling as replaceable because healthcare providers commonly already rely on established booking, identity, and e-health infrastructure. Med Flow therefore concentrates on connecting booking context, audio capture, local AI processing, review, persistence, and communication through clear component interfaces.

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{subsec:motivation}

Medical consultations often require doctors to divide attention between the patient conversation and documentation. This can reduce consultation quality and delay follow-up material.

Med Flow is motivated by the need for a privacy-conscious workflow where recording, transcription, draft generation, and follow-up communication reduce clerical friction while preserving the physician-review safeguards recommended for AI medical scribe workflows\refcite{2}.

\subsection{Objective}
\label{subsec:objective}

The objective is to design and implement a secure consultation-support system for doctors, patients, and administrators. The system records consultation audio, converts spoken dialogue into text, generates draft clinical content, and lets the doctor review, edit, approve, or reject the result.

After approval, the system supports PDF download and email-based follow-up communication while preserving role-based access to patient, doctor, and consultation information.

\subsection{Delimitation}
\label{subsec:delimitation}

The application is not intended to provide a complete medical administration system. It does not include full clinical record management, long-term clinical testing, user studies with external patients or healthcare providers, live medical deployment, advanced compliance certification, large-scale deployment, or full integration with existing healthcare systems.

The project focuses on supporting activities around an outpatient consultation. A local booking/scheduling flow can create consultation context inside the semester system, but a real deployment should be able to replace that part with an existing clinic, company, or public-sector scheduling service. In a Danish setting, this could include identity and login infrastructure such as MitID and NemLog-in rather than a custom identity flow for every healthcare provider\refcite{14,15}.

The generated transcription, summaries, suggestions, and PDF drafts are decision-support material. They are not autonomous medical outputs, and the approval boundary is kept explicit before information is processed further or shared with the patient.
