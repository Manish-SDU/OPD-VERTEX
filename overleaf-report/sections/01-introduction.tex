\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Med Flow was developed as part of the fourth-semester Software Engineering curriculum. It is a web-based consultation-support system for recording consultation audio, transcribing speech, generating draft clinical documentation, and supporting doctor review, PDF export, and follow-up communication. The project responds to the documented administrative burden created by electronic health record work, where documentation can consume a substantial part of the clinical day~[1].

The scope is deliberately focused on the consultation and post-consultation workflow. Scheduling is treated as an external component because healthcare providers commonly already rely on separate scheduling systems. Med Flow instead focuses on local processing, modular system design, and human-controlled AI assistance, so generated transcripts, suggestions, and documents remain reviewable decision-support rather than autonomous medical outputs~[2,3].

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{subsec:motivation}

Medical consultations often require doctors to divide attention between the patient conversation and documentation. This can reduce consultation quality and delay follow-up material.

Med Flow is motivated by the need for a privacy-conscious workflow where recording, transcription, draft generation, and follow-up communication support the doctor without removing clinical responsibility. The goal is to reduce clerical friction documented in outpatient EHR work~[1] while preserving the physician-review safeguards recommended for AI medical scribe workflows~[2].

\subsection{Objective}
\label{subsec:objective}

The objective is to design and implement a secure consultation-support system for doctors, patients, and administrators. The system records consultation audio, converts spoken dialogue into text, generates draft clinical content, and lets the doctor review, edit, approve, or reject the result.

After approval, the system supports PDF download and email-based follow-up communication while preserving role-based access to patient, doctor, and consultation information.

\subsection{Delimitation}
\label{subsec:delimitation}

The application is not intended to provide a complete medical administration system. It does not include full clinical record management, long-term clinical testing, user studies with external patients or healthcare providers, live medical deployment, advanced compliance certification, large-scale deployment, or full integration with existing healthcare systems.

The project focuses on supporting activities during and after an ongoing consultation. In the intended deployment context, healthcare providers are assumed to already rely on established scheduling systems. In line with the system's component-based architecture, scheduling is treated as an external concern that can be integrated through well-defined interfaces.

The generated transcription, summaries, suggestions, and PDF drafts are decision-support material for the doctor. They are not autonomous medical outputs. Final responsibility for reviewing, editing, approving, or declining generated content remains with the doctor before information is processed further or shared with the patient.
